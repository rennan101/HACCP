###### **What is HACCP?** 

###### Logo 

**Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point** is an international food safety management system designed to **prevent** , **reduce** , or **eliminate** hazards in food. Instead of relying on inspecting finished food products, HACCP aims to identify and control risks during production. Under Codex guidelines, HACCP is built upon **7 fundamental principles** to ensure food safety management: 

**1.Conduct a Hazard Analysis** 

- **2.Determine Critical Control Points (CCPs)** 

- **3.Establish Critical Limits** 

- **4.Establish Monitoring Procedures** 

**5.Establish Corrective Actions** 

- **6.Establish Verification Procedures** 

- **7.Establish Record-Keeping**

**Where does HACCP come from?** Logo _=— HACCP was originated in the 1960s through a collaboration between NASA, the **U.S. Army Laboratories** , and the **Pillsbury Company** . It was originally designed to guarantee 100% safe, crumb-free, and pathogen-free food for astronauts on early space missions. 

**1970s:** The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) adopted HACCP principles for commercial canned food manufacturing. 

**1990s:** International organisations, including the Codex Alimentarius Commissi on, adopted HACCP guidelines. 

**2000s:** HACCP became a strict, legally mandated requirement across many regions (including the UK, through Regulation (EC) 852/2004) 



###### **What are the hazards in food?** 

###### Logo 

A **food hazard** is something dangerous that could make food unsafe or unfit to eat. 

The food business MUST identify hazards at each stage of the processing flow so they can be removed or reduced to safe levels. 

There are three main types of food safety hazards: 



physical - involving objects getting into food 

chemical - involving chemical contamination 

microbiological - involving harmful bacteria 

Allergen – evolving the 12 allergens listed by the EU/UK government 

**Regulations** Logo __ The **Codex Alimentarius** (Latin for "Food Code") is a collection of internationally recognised food standards, guidelines, and codes of practice established by the FAO and WHO. 

**Codex Alimentarius** establishes the baseline food hygiene and safety standards that nations adopt to protect consumers and facilitate fair trade. As part of these standards, Codex mandates that food businesses implement a **HACCP-based system** . 

- While Codex defines **_what_** the general requirements are, HACCP acts as the practical **_how_** _-to_ framework to identify, evaluate, and control food safety hazards 

HACCP was introduced by **Regulation (EC) No 852/2004** on food hygiene. This law made HACCP-based procedures mandatory for food businesses in the UK. 

**EHO (Environmental Health Officer) can fine the business or shut the company down if the HACCP plan is not implemented.**

###### Logo 

###### **CCP Monitoring** 

To monitor Critical Control Points (CCPs) effectively in a HACCP system, we need to regularly observe control measures to ensure they remain within established critical limits. 

Our monitoring system MUST match the production records and reflect the immediate results. 

**The Technical/Quality team must be informed immediately in case of CCP deviations** 

A Non-Conformities/Incident Report MUST be recorded in case of deviation. 

###### **Environmental Monitoring** 

Environmental monitoring is important for controlling potential pathogens and indicates adherence to Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and housekeeping. Routine testing for generic organisms must be taken regularly to eliminate and reduce contamination to an acceptable level. 

The frequency of testing is based on risk, which will be determined by a Risk Assessment managed by the HACCP Team.

### **PRE-REQUISITE PROGRAMME** = **Prerequisite Programs (PRPs)** are the foundational hygiene, environmental, 

Logo 

and operational practices required before implementing a HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point) system. They establish a safe operating environment by managing general risks, so the HACCP team can focus on specific, critical hazards. PRPs key points: 

**1. Cleaning and Sanitation:** Procedures for regularly cleaning and sanitising surfaces, equipment, and utensils. 

**2. Personal Hygiene:** Requirements for handwashing, protective clothing, and health monitoring of employees. 

**3. Pest Control:** Preventative measures and regular inspections to keep the production area free of pests. 

**4. Equipment and Premises Maintenance:** Keeping facilities and machinery in good working order to prevent contamination or breakdowns. 

**5. Supplier Control:** Ensuring ingredients are sourced from reputable suppliers that meet safety standards. 

**6. Allergen Management:** Segregation and handling rules to protect customers from cross-contact. 

**7. Staff Training:** Ensuring all workers understand basic food hygiene and their role in safety.

###### **Corrective Actions / Action Plan** 

**Corrective actions** are steps to bring the process back under control, determine the cause of the deviation, and prevent recurrence. 

**For example** , if a cooking temperature falls below the critical limit in the Scrambled Egg production (< 86 °C), corrective actions MUST be taken immediately to bring the process back under control, also determine the cause of the deviation, and prevent recurrence. 

The quality tool **PDCA** can support the HACCP team in reviewing the process and reassessing the HACCP system to prevent recurrence of production deviations. 

**Plan:** Conduct your hazard analysis, identify Critical Control Points (CCPs), set critical limits, and establish monitoring procedures. 

**Do:** Implement the HACCP plan, train staff, and execute your control measures and operational routines. 

**Check:** Monitor the CCPs, review temperature logs, and conduct internal audits to ensure safety protocols are functioning as intended. 

**Act:** Take corrective actions when deviations occur, standardise successful improvements, and update the HACCP plan to prevent future issues

A HACCP team is a multidisciplinary group responsible for **developing** , **implementing** , and **maintaining** a food safety management system.
