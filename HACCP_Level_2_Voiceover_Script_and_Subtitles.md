### Slide 7: The 7 HACCP Principles
**Visual Reference:** Slide 7 – *The 7 HACCP Principles (Hazard Analysis, CCPs, Critical Limits, Monitoring, Corrective Actions, Verification, Record-Keeping)*

#### 🎙️ Voiceover Script (British English)
> "Let's walk through the Seven Principles of HACCP in detail.
> Principle One: Conduct a Hazard Analysis. We examine every single step in our dairy process to identify biological, chemical, physical, and allergen hazards.
> Principle Two: Determine Critical Control Points, or CCPs. These are the specific steps where a control measure is essential to prevent, eliminate, or reduce a hazard to an acceptable level.
> Principle Three: Establish Critical Limits. We define measurable boundaries—such as minimum pasteurisation temperatures and holding times—that separate safe food from unsafe food.
> Principle Four: Establish Monitoring Procedures. We set up continuous or scheduled checks to confirm that every CCP stays strictly within its critical limits.
> Principle Five: Establish Corrective Actions. If monitoring shows a deviation, we execute immediate, predefined actions to regain control and isolate non-compliant product.
> Principle Six: Establish Verification Procedures. Through audits, calibrations, and microbiological tests, we verify that our HACCP system is working effectively.
> And Principle Seven: Establish Record-Keeping. We maintain clear, tamper-proof logs and documents proving our safety controls have operated correctly."

**Visual Reference:** Slide 24 – *Corrective Actions / Action Plan – PDCA (Plan, Do, Check, Act)*

#### 🎙️ Voiceover Script (British English)
> "What happens when something goes wrong? That is where Corrective Actions come into play. A corrective action is a specific procedure taken when monitoring shows that a CCP has deviated from its critical limit. It has three aims: bring the process back under control, determine the root cause, and prevent recurrence.
> 
> For example, if a pasteuriser temperature drops below seventy-two degrees Celsius, the diverter automatically returns the milk to the balance tank. The operator logs the incident, quarantines any suspect product, and investigates the steam supply or heat exchanger.
> 
> We utilise the PDCA quality cycle to continuously refine our HACCP plan:
> Plan: Conduct hazard analyses and set critical limits.
> Do: Implement controls, train teams, and execute routines.
> Check: Monitor CCPs, review thermograph charts, and run audits.
> Act: Standardise fixes, update procedures, and prevent repeat deviations."
